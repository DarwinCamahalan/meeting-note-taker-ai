## Summary & Score

**Overall score: 46 / 100 — Thoughtful compliance *scaffolding* wrapped around an unresolved, potentially criminal, core: capturing a non-consenting third party's audio, with disclosure optional and defaulted OFF, and the authoritative legal analysis deferred to a document that does not yet exist.**

Cue's plan is unusually self-aware for a product in this category. It repeatedly names the right risks (two-party consent, GDPR, "cheating-tool" framing), makes several genuinely good privacy decisions (raw audio never persisted, model-training opt-out **on by default**, region-pinned data, honest content-protection limitations, an explicit "we will not hide the process from the OS/EDR" ethical line, PCI SAQ-A scoping, accessibility as a first-class constraint), and treats consent/disclosed-mode as an explicit Phase-1 launch blocker.

But the substance behind that scaffolding is missing or mis-defaulted in ways that a due-diligence buyer must treat as blocking:

1. **The core data flow captures and transcribes the *other party's* voice.** In ~11–12 U.S. all-party-consent ("two-party") states and under many non-U.S. regimes, doing that without every party's consent is a criminal wiretapping/eavesdropping offense — and under GDPR the other party is a data subject for whom the plan establishes **no lawful basis, no notice, and no way to exercise rights.**
2. **Disclosed mode is optional and `disclosed` defaults to `false`**, and there is no jurisdiction-gated hard block — the architecture's default posture is the legally risky one.
3. **Every hard legal question is forwarded to `90-legal-compliance.md`, which does not exist.** The Acceptable-Use Policy, jurisdictional consent matrix, exact consent copy, DPA templates, and retention legal bases are all "owned by the legal/compliance doc" — i.e., unwritten. The compliance posture is currently a promise, not an artifact.

The intentions and structure are worth real credit, which keeps this out of the 20s. The unresolved criminal-liability surface, the wrong default, and the absence of the actual governing document cap it below 50 until remediated.

## Top risks

1. **Criminal wiretapping exposure (L1).** Capturing the other party's audio without all-party consent is a crime in ~12 U.S. states and abroad; disclosed mode is optional and defaults off. This is the single existential legal risk and is currently unmitigated by architecture.
2. **The governing legal document does not exist (L2).** Every hard question is deferred to `90-legal-compliance.md`, which is unwritten — so no compliance claim in the plan is presently verifiable or enforceable.
3. **No GDPR lawful basis for the non-consenting third party (L3).** Undefined controller/processor roles + no notice + no rights mechanism = 4%-of-turnover exposure and no lawful EU launch.
4. **Product/positioning contradiction on live interview assistance (L4).** The rejected "cheating tool" is, functionally, what the interview persona uses; unresolved in product, not just marketing.
5. **Processing begins (Phase 1) before DPAs/ZDR terms with the four AI sub-processors are in place (L6).** A standalone GDPR/CCPA violation independent of the consent problem.

## Prioritized remediations

1. **Write `90-legal-compliance.md` and have outside counsel review it — before any production capture (Phase 1 gate).** It must contain the jurisdictional consent matrix, the Acceptable-Use Policy, the sub-processor/DPA register, controller/processor determinations, third-party DSAR procedures, breach-notification runbook, and law-enforcement policy. Nothing else on this list is safe until this exists. (Resolves L2; unblocks L1, L3, L7, L8, L9, L13.)
2. **Re-architect consent to strictest-default.** Default `disclosed = true`/require disclosure for any third-party capture; hard-block or force-disclose in all-party contexts; require a logged attestation to downgrade to one-party; capture evidence of disclosure to the *other* parties, not just the user's click. (Resolves L5, L14; mitigates L1.)
3. **Execute DPAs + zero-retention / no-training addenda with Anthropic, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, and Voyage, and publish a sub-processor list — before Phase 1 capture.** Verify STT vendors contractually and technically do not retain the third party's voice. (Resolves L6.)
4. **Resolve the interview-assistance contradiction in product.** Enumerate hard-prohibited contexts in the AUP (interviews without disclosure, proctored/licensing exams, clearance interviews, legal proceedings); gate `interview_live` behind a disclosure attestation; restate the Priya persona to disclosed/prep-only. (Resolves L4, L7.)
5. **Define the GDPR/CCPA data-subject model, including third parties.** Controller/processor roles, external privacy notice covering third parties, third-party DSAR intake, and minimized third-party retention (pull content out of the 400-day audit tier). (Resolves L3, L8.)
6. **Ship tamper-evident consent records from day one** (append-only, hash-chained; store exact copy shown + jurisdiction basis + disclosure evidence). (Resolves L12.)
7. **Draft the baseline legal pages** — ToS (warranty disclaimer, limitation of liability, no-professional-advice, misuse indemnity, accuracy disclaimer), privacy policy, cookie/consent, DMCA, user-upload rights warranty — and complete trademark clearance. (Resolves L11, L15.)
8. **Harden platform/distribution posture.** Surface conferencing-platform recording rules at session start; obtain counsel's opinion on picker-enumeration suppression; keep the honest "we do not hide the process" line; maintain a notarization-revocation contingency for the auto-update channel. (Resolves L10.)
9. **Pull enterprise-trust artifacts earlier where cheap.** DPA template + SOC 2 readiness/Type I ahead of Phase 3; do not enable admin cross-member session visibility until the governing policy defines its basis. (Addresses L9.)
10. **Convert the accessibility strength into a defensible claim.** WCAG 2.2 AA conformance + published VPAT/accessibility statement (ADA Title III / EU Accessibility Act). Preserve SAQ-A discipline. (Reinforces L16.)
