# Consolidated Audit Summary — Cue

> Status: Draft · Owner: Architecture · Last updated: 2026-07-29 · Related: [Master index](../README.md) · [Roadmap](../80-roadmap.md)

This document consolidates the five independent due-diligence audits of the Cue planning set into one scorecard, one merged severity-ranked findings table, and a single prioritized remediation roadmap. It is the recommended entry point before any build decision. Individual audits carry the full reasoning; this summary de-duplicates overlapping findings and sequences the fixes.

**Bottom line:** The plan is architecturally strong and unusually self-aware, but it is gated by an unresolved legal core (third-party recording consent) and carries several load-bearing contradictions that two teams would resolve differently if not reconciled before code. The legal and consent items below are **hard launch blockers**, not backlog.

---

## Scorecard

| Dimension | Score | One-line verdict |
|-----------|:-----:|------------------|
| [Architecture & Code Quality](04-architecture-quality-audit.md) | **78 / 100** | Mature, well-operationalized design; held back by cross-doc contradictions on the hot-path transport and v1 scope in a plan that promises "contracts before code." |
| [Security](01-security-audit.md) | **72 / 100** | Above-median security *design*, undermined by a missing supply-chain program for a code-signed auto-updater, transcript data protected only at the volume level, and ungoverned voice egress to STT vendors. |
| [Scalability & Reliability](02-scalability-reliability-audit.md) | **68 / 100** | Right shape and honest risk register, undercut by a single regional Redis on the hot path, a capacity model sized on global (not per-region) demand, and an optimistic, inconsistently-measured latency SLO. |
| [Financial / Unit Economics](03-financial-profitability-audit.md) | **58 / 100** | Correct token arithmetic on a business layer that rests on a 7.5× overage-rate contradiction, adverse selection into the loss-making tail, optimistic churn, and a placeholder break-even 2–3× too low. |
| Legal, Compliance & Responsible Use _(audit removed — out of scope)_ | **46 / 100** | Thoughtful scaffolding around a potentially criminal core: capturing a non-consenting third party's audio, disclosure optional and defaulted off, with the authoritative legal document unwritten. |

## Merged findings (severity-ranked, de-duplicated)

Findings that appeared in more than one audit are merged and tagged with all source IDs. Source keys: **L** = legal, **S** = security, **SR** = scalability, **F** = finance, **A** = architecture.

| Severity | Area | Finding | Recommendation | Source |
|:--------:|------|---------|----------------|:------:|
| **Critical** | Recording-consent / wiretapping | Cue captures both sides of the call — the other party's voice — with disclosed mode optional and `disclosed` defaulting to `false`. In ~12 all-party-consent U.S. states and much of the EU this is a criminal offense; the consent record captures only the user's consent. | Treat all-party consent as the global default; require an affirmative disclosed/consent step or hard-block capture; record immutable evidence that disclosure was made. | L1, L5, L14 |
| **Critical** | Governing legal document missing | Every hard legal question (AUP, jurisdiction matrix, consent copy, DPA templates, retention bases) is deferred to `90-legal-compliance.md`, which does not exist. No compliance claim is presently verifiable or enforceable. | Author and have outside counsel review `90-legal-compliance.md` before any production capture; it is the prerequisite that unblocks most other legal items. | L2, L7 |
| **Critical** | GDPR lawful basis (third party) | The non-consenting other participant is a data subject; the plan establishes no Art. 6 lawful basis, no Art. 13/14 notice, and no rights mechanism. Controller/processor roles are undefined. | Define controller/processor model; require disclosure/consent for EU-touching sessions or block; publish an external privacy notice covering third parties; add a third-party DSAR intake. | L3, L8 |
| **Critical** | AI sub-processor DPAs / voice egress | Raw audio (potentially biometric) streams to Deepgram/AssemblyAI; transcripts + resume to Anthropic/Voyage. DPAs + zero-retention/no-training are treated as Phase 3, and retention control is a fragile per-request flag rather than a contractual account-level guarantee. | Execute DPAs + ZDR/no-training with all four vendors **before Phase 1 capture**; verify STT no-retention contractually and technically; maintain a sub-processor register; assert the no-retention flag in CI on every provider call. | L6, S-03 |
| **Critical** | Overage / tail margin | Overage is configured at **$0.02/min** in Stripe but modelled at **~$0.15/min** in unit economics — a 7.5× contradiction that invalidates the "heavy users become more profitable" thesis. | Reconcile to one number before Pro GA; to achieve the intended ~10× COGS defense, price overage ~$0.13–0.15/min and validate against willingness-to-pay. | F-01 |
| **Critical** | Redis hot path SPOF | Per-frame `XADD` implies ~225k+ audio ops/sec at Growth peak on a single regional Redis that also holds the Claude token bucket, sessions, and WS offsets; failover (10–30s) stalls every live session in the region, and ops rate/failover are never modeled. | Model Redis ops/sec per scenario; batch or bypass Redis for raw audio; split the token-bucket/counters Redis from the audio-stream Redis; add Redis throughput + failover to the core load suite. | SR-01, SR-07 |
| **High** | Software supply chain | No dependency scanning, SBOM, provenance, secret-scanning gate, or verified native addons — yet builds are code-signed, notarized, and auto-pushed to every user via `autoDownload`. A single poisoned dependency becomes a trusted push. | Stand up a supply-chain program (frozen lockfile, advisory scanning as merge gate, SBOM per release, build provenance, secret scanning, hash-pinned native addons) before enabling auto-download. | S-01 |
| **High** | Data at rest / PII | The most sensitive data (transcript segments — third-party meeting content) is protected only by volume-level KMS encryption; application/envelope encryption is deferred. Replicas, snapshots, logical dumps, and insider access bypass volume encryption. | Promote per-org envelope encryption of transcript/summary/RAG content to a launch requirement; independently encrypt logical backups. | S-02 |
| **High** | Auto-update integrity | The update manifest is fetched from the same origin as the installer and is not independently signed; Windows signature verification only holds if `publisherName`/`verifyUpdateCodeSignature` is explicitly configured. | Independently sign the manifest (or adopt a TUF-style feed) with a key distinct from R2 credentials; pin and test Windows publisher verification and macOS stapling; add tamper-rejection CI tests. | S-04 |
| **High** | Responsible-use contradiction | The plan rejects "cheating tool" framing yet the interview persona receives live cues during real interviews on a screen-share-invisible overlay — functionally the rejected use. Internal contradiction, not just positioning. | Hard-prohibit in the AUP: interviews without disclosure, proctored/licensing exams, clearance interviews, legal proceedings; gate `interview_live` behind a disclosure attestation; restate the persona to prep/disclosed note-taking. | L4 |
| **High** | Hot-path transport contradiction | The authoritative architecture doc labels the `ws-gateway`↔`ai-orchestrator` edge gRPC/WS while backend-services says Redis streams; the cue return hop is not budgeted. Two teams will build two systems on the product's defining latency edge. | Pick one transport; make docs 02 and 20 agree; add explicit per-hop budget lines (including the return path) and validate empirically. Close backend Open-Q #1 before build. | A01 |
| **High** | Capacity model (global vs regional) | Fleet, STT lease pool, and Claude TPM are sized from a single smoothed global peak, but US and EU are region-pinned stacks with no cross-region failover; each must be sized to its own regional peak. | Re-derive capacity per region from the local business-hours curve; state the US/EU split; size each region's gateway fleet, STT pool, and Claude quota to its own peak + headroom. | SR-02 |
| **High** | Anthropic quota coordination | The Claude admission token bucket is account-level but lives in per-region Redis; two stacks drawing on a shared account quota can jointly exceed it and trigger 429/529 storms — the exact failure it exists to prevent. | Provision separate Anthropic orgs/keys per region, or implement a genuinely global cross-region token bucket; do the same for STT ceilings; extend the 429-storm load test to the two-region case. | SR-05 |
| **High** | Latency SLO validity | The `<1.2s p95` is derived by summing per-stage p95s (not a valid e2e p95), starts the clock after endpointing, excludes client network for non-US/EU users, and drifts across docs; the split point to attribute client vs server latency does not yet exist. | Publish one reconciled, empirically-validated e2e budget with an explicit start point; build the trace split before committing the SLO; separate server-controllable from user-network latency; fold cold-cache cues into the reported p95. | SR-03, SR-11, SR-14 |
| **High** | Availability vs DR posture | The 99.9% SLO coexists with a design where a regional outage drops all that region's users to snapshot-restore RTO with no failover; failover times are cited but never quantified against the budget. | Quantify Aurora/Redis failover and DR RTO/RPO; then either lower the published SLO to what single-region delivers or fund an in-region/second-EU hot-standby for the live control plane. | SR-04 |
| **High** | Adverse selection into loss tail | The 72% margin assumes 300 min/mo, but the personas who pay $20 (sales, CS, recruiters, job seekers) are the heavy users the sensitivity grid shows going margin-negative near the cap. | Model margin against the *paid* usage distribution, not a whole-base average; segment expected minutes by persona; stress the heavy tail with the corrected overage rate. | F-02 |
| **High** | Churn / LTV / break-even | LTV leans on an implausible 5%/mo churn for transactional interview-prep users; the free-tier "self-funding" proof is dimensionally invalid; and the $120k/mo opex is 2–3× too low and excludes all S&M. | Split churn/LTV by persona; rebuild free-tier economics on a cohort basis; replace the placeholder opex with a bottom-up build and present a true cash break-even including CAC. | F-03, F-04, F-05 |
| **Medium** | Sonnet pricing cliff | Premium-event costs and the sensitivity grid use Sonnet intro pricing ($2/$10) that expires 2026-08-31 — before likely GA. Post-cliff pricing is $3/$15. | Make post-intro Sonnet ($3/$15) the base case; show intro pricing only as expired upside; confirm the router's post-cliff eligibility tightening is specced. | F-07 |
| **Medium** | STT cost basis | The dominant COGS line assumes a single mixed mono stream (≈1× wall-clock), never stated; two-channel STT for diarization quality would roughly double the largest cost line. | Make the single-mixed-stream assumption explicit and tie it to the diarization approach; re-run COGS at 2× STT as a downside; lock a committed STT contract early. | F-10, F-08 |
| **Medium** | v1 scope & source-of-truth contradictions | Three "what ships in v1" contradictions (billing-webhooks module vs service; `apps/api` vs `services/api`; `ai-orchestrator` lean vs NestJS) plus two divergent `tsconfig.base.json` — forks in a plan that sells single-source-of-truth. | One canonical statement each; unify on `packages/config/tsconfig.base.json`; enforce DTO codegen direction from `api` Zod schemas in CI. Reconcile before code. | A02, A03, A04, A05, A09 |
| **Medium** | pgvector filtered recall | A single shared HNSW index with an `org_id` pre-filter cannot truly "filter first"; recall/latency degrade unpredictably at multi-tenant scale, but the hot-path budget assumes them away. | Validate filtered-HNSW recall AND latency under a realistic multi-tenant corpus; add a recall assertion to the load test; plan per-tenant partial/partitioned indexes before Growth. | SR-06 |
| **Medium** | Embedding model divergence | Docs disagree on the embedding model (`voyage-3.5` / `voyage-3.5-lite` / `voyage-3-large`); mixing query and doc embedding spaces silently degrades retrieval. | Pin one embedding model end-to-end; reconcile the name across docs; add a guard test that fails if query/doc models diverge. | SR-09 |
| **Medium** | No e2e latency release gate | The <1.2s SLO is validated only per-layer; no owned test exercises utterance → painted overlay token on real hardware as a release gate. | Add a staging e2e latency gate on representative hardware wired into the load-test release gate; own the client-side budget split. | A07 |
| **Medium** | Internal crypto & secrets | The JWT signing key is a raw secret in Secrets Manager (not KMS); ElastiCache/Service Connect TLS and Redis AUTH are unspecified though interim transcripts and audio frames traverse Redis; the WS ticket rides the connection query string. | Use KMS asymmetric signing (or tightly scope + rotate); require ElastiCache encryption/AUTH and internal TLS; move the WS ticket to a subprotocol/first-message frame; reclassify Redis as sensitive-data-bearing. | S-05, S-06, S-07 |
| **Medium** | Stage-inappropriate breadth | A standalone `entitlements` service, ClickHouse, dual-STT failover, and dual-region residency are day-1 commitments, applying the "extract later" rule inconsistently and raising ops/COGS ahead of PMF. | Apply extract-later consistently (consider `entitlements` as a module in `api` for v1); defer ClickHouse and eu-west-1 until a concrete driver exists; gate dual-STT behind a resilience milestone. | A06 |
| **Low** | Consent-record integrity | The legally load-bearing consent row is not yet tamper-evident/hash-chained and does not capture the exact copy shown or evidence of the other party's disclosure. | Ship append-only, hash-chained consent records from Phase 1, capturing `policy_version`, exact copy rendered, jurisdiction basis, and disclosure evidence. | L12 |
| **Low** | Baseline legal pages & IP | No ToS (warranty disclaimer, limitation of liability, no-professional-advice, misuse indemnity), privacy/cookie/DMCA pages, or upload-rights warranty; "Cue" trademark is uncleared. | Draft the baseline legal pages and an accuracy disclaimer; add an upload-rights warranty; complete trademark clearance before brand spend. | L11, L15 |
| **Low (positive)** | Preserve genuine strengths | PCI SAQ-A scoping, `training_opt_out` default true, raw audio never persisted, region pinning, honest content-protection limitations, and accessibility as a first-class constraint are real assets. | Keep SAQ-A discipline; obtain WCAG 2.2 AA conformance + a published VPAT to make the accessibility claim defensible. | L16 |

## Prioritized remediation roadmap

The top ~12 actions to take before or while building, ordered. Each is tagged with the [80-roadmap.md](../80-roadmap.md) phase it belongs to. **Items 1–4 are gating** — no production audio capture is safe until they are complete.

| # | Action | Phase | Gating? |
|:-:|--------|:-----:|:-------:|
| 1 | **Write `90-legal-compliance.md` and have outside counsel review it** — jurisdictional consent matrix, AUP, DPA/sub-processor register, controller/processor determinations, third-party DSAR procedures, breach-notification runbook. Nothing else legal is verifiable until this exists. (L2, L7) | **Phase 1** (before first capture) | **YES** |
| 2 | **Re-architect consent to strictest-default** — default to all-party rules, require disclosed mode / logged attestation to downgrade, capture evidence of the other party's disclosure, and ship append-only hash-chained consent records from day one. (L1, L5, L12, L14) | **Phase 1** | **YES** |
| 3 | **Execute DPAs + zero-retention / no-training addenda with Anthropic, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, and Voyage, and publish a sub-processor list** — verify STT vendors do not retain the third party's voice, contractually and technically; assert the flag in CI. (L6, S-03) | **Phase 1** | **YES** |
| 4 | **Resolve the interview-assistance contradiction in product** — enumerate hard-prohibited contexts in the AUP, gate `interview_live` behind a disclosure attestation, and restate the interview persona to prep/disclosed note-taking only. (L4) | **Phase 1** | **YES** |
| 5 | **Reconcile the "contracts before code" contradictions before writing code** — one canonical decision each for the hot-path transport (02 vs 20), billing-webhooks placement, `services/api` path, `ai-orchestrator` structure, and a single `tsconfig.base.json`; pin one embedding model end-to-end; enforce DTO codegen direction in CI. (A01, A02–A05, A09, SR-09) | **Phase 1** (design/contracts) | No |
| 6 | **Quantify and de-risk the Redis hot path and derive capacity per region** — model Redis ops/sec per scenario, batch or bypass Redis for raw audio, split the token-bucket Redis from the audio-stream Redis, and size each region's fleet/STT pool/Claude quota to its own peak. (SR-01, SR-02, SR-07) | **Phase 1** design → **Phase 4** validation | No |
| 7 | **Add the end-to-end latency release gate on real hardware** and publish one reconciled, empirically-validated latency budget with an explicit start point and a server-vs-client split. (A07, SR-03, SR-11, SR-14) | **Phase 1** | No |
| 8 | **Make sensitive-content envelope encryption a launch requirement** — per-org KMS-wrapped encryption of transcript/summary/RAG content; independently encrypt logical backups. (S-02) | **Phase 1 → Phase 2** | No |
| 9 | **Stand up the software supply-chain program before enabling auto-download** — frozen lockfile, advisory scanning as a merge gate, SBOM per release, build provenance, secret scanning, and hash-pinned native addons. (S-01) | **Phase 2** (before auto-update GA) | No |
| 10 | **Harden the auto-update path** — independently sign the manifest (or TUF), pin and test Windows publisher verification and macOS stapling, add tamper-rejection CI tests; fix the WS-ticket transport and internal crypto (KMS signing, ElastiCache TLS/AUTH). (S-04, S-05, S-06, S-07) | **Phase 2** | No |
| 11 | **Reconcile the overage rate and rebuild the business model** — one overage number set to clear COGS, margin re-modelled against the *paid* usage distribution, churn/LTV split by persona, a bottom-up opex and true cash break-even, conventional margin restatement, and post-intro Sonnet pricing as the base case. (F-01, F-02, F-03, F-04, F-05, F-06, F-07) | **Phase 2** (before Pro GA) | No |
| 12 | **Reconcile availability posture with the SLO and make admission control genuinely regional** — quantify Aurora/Redis failover and DR RTO/RPO, then either lower the SLO or fund an in-region hot-standby; provision per-region Anthropic/STT quota; validate pgvector filtered recall at scale. (SR-04, SR-05, SR-06) | **Phase 4** (design decided earlier) | No |

> **Sequencing note.** The roadmap already calls consent a Phase-1 launch blocker; items 1–4 make that concrete and must precede the first production capture. Items 5–7 are "contracts before code" reconciliations that should land during Phase 1 design so two teams do not build divergent systems. Items 8–10 track the Phase 2 monetization/auto-update surface. Item 11 must be settled before Pro GA. Item 12 is Phase 4 scale work whose design decisions (regional quota, DR standby) should be made early even if executed late.
