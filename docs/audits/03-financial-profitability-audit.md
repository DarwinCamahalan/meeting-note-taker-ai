## Summary & score

**Overall score: 58 / 100** — The per-session COGS token math is arithmetically correct and unusually well-labelled (assumptions vs. canonical prices), but the business-economics layer rests on several load-bearing assumptions that are optimistic or internally contradictory — most critically a 7.5× contradiction in the overage rate that invalidates the model's entire heavy-user margin-defense thesis.

The good: the Claude token/cache arithmetic in §3 reproduces exactly ($0.60 → $0.173 with caching; $0.0148/min all-in), assumptions are explicitly flagged as estimates, STT is correctly named the #1 unknown, and the §8 sensitivity grid honestly shows the margin cliff is driven by minutes, not model mix.

The problem: (1) the overage rate that supposedly makes heavy users *more* profitable is configured at $0.02/min in Stripe but modelled at $0.15/min in unit economics; (2) the paying population is structurally skewed toward the high-usage tail where margin goes negative (adverse selection); (3) LTV leans on a 5%/mo churn that is implausible for the transactional interview-prep persona; (4) the free-tier "self-funding" proof is dimensionally invalid; and (5) the $120k/mo fixed-opex break-even is 2–3× too low for the described team and excludes all S&M. Individually survivable, collectively they mean the headline "72% Pro margin, break-even ~7,400 subs" would not survive investor due diligence without restatement.

## Top risks

1. **Overage-rate contradiction (F-01).** The margin-defense mechanism is priced 7.5× lower in Stripe than in the economics model; at $0.02/min the heavy-user tail is a near-loss, not a profit centre.
2. **Adverse selection into the loss-making tail (F-02).** The personas who pay are the heavy users the model shows going margin-negative; the 300-min "average" likely does not describe the paid base.
3. **Optimistic churn → optimistic LTV/CAC (F-03).** Transactional interview-prep churn could halve LTV and cut the sustainable CAC ceiling in half.
4. **Understated break-even (F-04, F-05).** A dimensionally-flawed free-tier proof plus a placeholder $120k/mo opex (2–3× too low, S&M excluded) understate the real path to profitability by a wide margin.
5. **Cost-base fragility (F-06, F-08, F-10).** Margin is sensitive to the true Stripe blended rate, the cache hit rate / prefix size, and — most of all — whether STT is one stream or two; none are locked.

---

## Prioritized remediations

1. **Reconcile the overage rate to one number** across [71](../71-unit-economics.md) and [51](../51-payments-stripe.md), set to clear COGS with real margin (~$0.13–0.15/min if the 10× defense is intended), before Pro GA. (F-01)
2. **Re-model margin against the paid usage distribution**, not the whole-base average; segment expected minutes by acquired persona and stress the heavy tail with the *corrected* overage rate. (F-02)
3. **Split churn and LTV by persona** (recurring vs. transactional) and re-derive the CAC ceiling and payback from cohort data before committing marketing spend. (F-03)
4. **Rebuild the free-tier and break-even models bottom-up**: cohort/lifetime free-tier math, a fully-loaded blended CAC that includes the free subsidy, a realistic headcount-driven opex, and a true cash break-even including S&M. (F-04, F-05)
5. **Restate margins conventionally** (COGS/gross revenue, Stripe fee in COGS) using a blended international payment-processing rate. (F-06)
6. **Re-base all Sonnet-dependent costs on post-intro pricing** ($3/$15) as the launch base case. (F-07)
7. **Add sensitivities for cache-hit-rate and prefix size, and re-derive Team COGS** with its actual larger prefix; wire cache-hit-rate into the margin dashboard. (F-08)
8. **Price a metering-leakage/trial-abuse COGS line** and define abuse thresholds + an overage-reporting reconciliation SLA. (F-09)
9. **Make the single-mixed-stream STT assumption explicit** and re-run COGS at 2× STT as a downside; lock a committed STT contract. (F-10)
10. **Fix cross-doc consistency** (single ARPU figure, derived Enterprise COGS, reproducible sensitivity grid). (F-11, F-12, F-13)
